Who counts as a second look

A rule, not a model. A household is in the group only when all three are true.

  1. FEMA verified the damage. An inspection came back and found real or personal property loss above $0.
  2. FEMA denied the aid anyway. The household was marked ineligible for Individuals and Households Program help.
  3. The reason is fixable. Missing documents (code 4), FEMA lost contact (code 3), or "other" reasons (codes 7, 9) where ownership or occupancy was never verified.

How we kept the model honest

FEMA's eligibility is close to a rule, so a naive model scores near-perfect and learns nothing. We train only on households with verified damage, use only what FEMA knew before deciding, and test on whole disasters the model never saw.

What drives the prediction

Permutation importance: how much average precision drops when a feature is shuffled.

How Granite writes the callback

      

        IBM's loan-approval lab pairs a risk model with policy retrieval and Granite to explain why a bank said yes or no.
        We run that pattern in reverse: the model finds households FEMA turned away, and Granite explains why the denial looks fixable.
